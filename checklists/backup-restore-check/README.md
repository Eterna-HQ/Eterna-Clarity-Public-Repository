# Can You Actually Restore Your Backup?

A small proof check for testing whether one real piece of information can actually be restored from a backup and used.

[Use the browser check](https://eternaclarity.com/resources/backup-restore-check/) · [Use the plain checklist](CHECK.md) · [See a fictional example](EXAMPLE.md)

## What this is

Choose **one real item or dataset** that matters. Restore it from the backup mechanism into a safe test location, then verify that you can open it and complete one real task with the restored copy.

Record:

- what you intended to restore;
- which backup source or service you used;
- the restore point or version;
- the safe test location;
- who performed the test;
- whether the restore completed;
- whether the restored information opened correctly;
- whether one real task could be completed;
- anything missing, corrupted or confusing; and
- the next action.

## Keep the test safe

Do not overwrite live or production information merely to prove a restore. Use a safe test location when possible, and follow the instructions of the relevant provider, IT administrator or qualified professional when restoration could affect active systems.

Do **not** put passwords, recovery codes, encryption keys or other secrets into this check.

## What success means

A successful test proves only the **specific item, restore point, method and task that you tested**. It does not prove that every backup is complete or that every system can be recovered.

## Important limits

This is a practical restore-proof resource, not a disaster-recovery plan, cyber-security assessment, ransomware-recovery procedure or guarantee that a backup is complete or trustworthy.

See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the Canadian guidance used to define the problem and boundaries.
