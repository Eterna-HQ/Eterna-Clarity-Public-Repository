# If Someone Leaves Today, What Access Has to Change?

A practical check for making sure a business still controls its accounts, information and recovery paths when an employee, contractor or other collaborator leaves or changes roles.

[Use the browser check](https://eternaclarity.com/resources/access-handover-check/) · [Use the plain checklist](CHECK.md) · [See a fictional example](EXAMPLE.md)

## What this is

Work through the areas that commonly become unclear during an offboarding or role change:

- business accounts and sign-in access;
- administrator ownership;
- MFA and recovery ownership;
- files and business information;
- devices, keys, badges or hardware tokens;
- shared credentials that must be changed through the approved credential manager;
- automations and integrations running under the person's identity;
- vendor or service-provider portals; and
- the final proof that another authorized person can still perform a real business task.

This checklist should record **status**, not secrets.

## Keep secrets out

Do **not** enter passwords, recovery codes, API keys, PINs, private keys or authentication secrets. Record that the access path was transferred or changed, and use the organization's approved credential-management process for the secret itself.

## Proof step

After the planned access changes, have another authorized person perform one real task that depends on the transferred accounts or information. If they cannot do it, the offboarding is not operationally complete.

## Important limits

This is an operational access-handover check. It is not employment, legal, privacy, cyber-security or identity-governance advice and does not determine which records or accounts an organization is legally required to retain.
