# Family Emergency Document Checklist

**Use:** mark only what applies to your household. “Ready” means another household member or someone you trust could find or use the current information without relying on your phone or memory.

## Three readiness needs

- **Know where it is** — the current or original record has a known location.
- **Keep the useful part accessible** — the phone number, reference number, short summary or appropriate copy that helps in the first hours can be reached quickly.
- **Protect the original** — sensitive originals stay in their normal secure home unless there is a real reason to move them.

> Do not write passwords, PINs, recovery codes, full credentials or unnecessary account secrets on this checklist. In a “where to find it” field, record a safe location or pointer — not the secret itself.

## If you only have 15 minutes

Start with the information most likely to matter in the first hours:

1. current emergency contacts and a meeting / pickup plan;
2. medications, allergies and essential medical equipment;
3. health coverage and insurance provider / policy information;
4. where current identity and applicable legal documents are kept;
5. care arrangements for children, dependants, pets or service animals; and
6. safe exits plus the location of the emergency kit.

That first pass is useful on its own. Come back for the rest when you have time.

## Contacts & coordination

- [ ] **Primary household emergency contacts** — Keep current names and phone numbers or another reliable way to reach them; anyone who may use the plan should know where it is.
- [ ] **Out-of-area contact** — Keep one current person outside your immediate area who household members can contact if local people are separated.
- [ ] **Meeting / reunification locations** — Record the places household members should use if home is unavailable or people are separated.
- [ ] **Trusted person who can find key information** — Someone you trust should know the plan exists and where to start if you are unavailable.
- [ ] **Local emergency / non-emergency information** — Keep only the local numbers or official information sources that are genuinely useful where you live.
## Health & care

- [ ] **Current medication list** — Keep a current, readable list accessible. Include the medication name, dosage/frequency and purpose when useful; this does not replace prescriptions or medical records.
- [ ] **Allergies and critical health information** — Keep the minimum information another household member, trusted person or caregiver may need quickly, and update it when health circumstances change.
- [ ] **Medical devices, equipment or essential supplies** — Know what is needed, where it is, how it is used or moved, and any power or supply needs that matter during a disruption.
- [ ] **Doctor, clinic and pharmacy contacts** — Keep current phone numbers or other reliable contact details if these providers matter to the household.
- [ ] **Care, accessibility or support instructions** — Write down the practical information someone would need to support a household member safely if normal help is disrupted.
- [ ] **Health card / coverage location** — Know where the current card or official record is. Keep only the minimum useful reference details accessible.
## Identity & legal

- [ ] **Passports and government-issued identification** — Know where current originals are kept. Use a copy only when it is useful and appropriate; a copy does not replace the original.
- [ ] **Birth, citizenship or immigration documents** — Know the secure location of applicable originals; avoid carrying originals unnecessarily in a general emergency folder.
- [ ] **Will, power of attorney, personal directive or similar document** — If applicable, someone you trust should know what exists and where the current signed or official document is kept. This checklist does not create legal authority.
- [ ] **Custody, guardianship or consent documents** — If applicable, know where the current document is kept and keep the practical information needed for emergency care or authorized pickup easy to find.
## Insurance & financial references

- [ ] **Home / tenant / vehicle insurance** — Keep the current insurer, policy reference and claims phone number easy to find; the full policy can stay in its normal secure location.
- [ ] **Other relevant insurance** — For life, health, travel, pet or other applicable coverage, keep enough information to identify the current provider and policy.
- [ ] **Banking and essential financial contacts** — Know which institutions matter and where current records are kept. If you keep a reference accessible, use only what is actually needed; do not record passwords, PINs or recovery codes here.
## Children, dependants & pets

- [ ] **School / daycare emergency and pickup arrangements** — If applicable, keep current school/daycare contact information and the authorized pickup plan easy to find.
- [ ] **Dependant care instructions** — Keep current practical care information for children, older relatives or other dependants someone may need to support.
- [ ] **Support network for disability or special needs** — If applicable, identify the people, equipment, supplies and practical instructions that matter when normal support is disrupted.
- [ ] **Pet / service-animal identification and care information** — Keep current identification, veterinarian details, medications, vaccination/licence references and other useful care information.
- [ ] **Pet / service-animal evacuation destination** — Know where the animal can go and what transport, carrier or accommodation arrangements are needed.
## Home & evacuation basics

- [ ] **Safe exits / evacuation route** — Household members should know the relevant exits and how to leave the area; follow current local authority instructions during an actual emergency.
- [ ] **Emergency kit / grab-and-go kit location** — Everyone who may need the kit should know where it is kept.
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher and key safety equipment locations** — Know where the equipment is. This checklist does not replace training, product instructions or emergency-service guidance.
- [ ] **Water, electrical, gas / heating and other utility controls** — Know where the controls are and who to contact. Do not improvise shutoff procedures during an emergency.
- [ ] **Landlord, building management or property contact** — If applicable, keep a current phone number or other reliable way to reach the person or service responsible.
- [ ] **Local hazards and official alerts** — Know which hazards are realistic where you live and which official sources provide warnings or instructions.

## Ten-minute test

- [ ] Someone you trust can find the current emergency contacts.
- [ ] They can find the medication / critical-care information that applies.
- [ ] They can identify current insurance and the right number to call.
- [ ] They know where relevant identity or legal originals are kept.
- [ ] They can understand child, dependant, pet or support arrangements that apply.
- [ ] They can do this without using your phone or asking you where everything is.

If one of these fails, fix the path to the information before making more copies.

## Review when life changes — and occasionally when it does not

- [ ] **Contact changes:** Phone number, address, trusted contact, provider or emergency contact changes.
- [ ] **Health changes:** Medication, allergy, condition, medical-device, care or accessibility needs change.
- [ ] **Household changes:** Someone joins or leaves the household; a dependant or caregiver arrangement changes.
- [ ] **School / care changes:** School, daycare, pickup authorization, custody or support arrangements change.
- [ ] **Insurance changes:** A policy renews, provider changes, coverage changes or claim information changes.
- [ ] **Identity / legal changes:** Passport or ID renews; a legal document is created, amended, revoked or moved.
- [ ] **Pet changes:** Pet medication, veterinarian, licence, microchip, boarding or evacuation arrangements change.
- [ ] **Home / move changes:** Address, building, utility, landlord, emergency-kit location or evacuation route changes.
- [ ] **After an emergency or drill:** Something was hard to find, unclear, out of date or inaccessible when tested.
- [ ] **Periodic refresh:** Even if nothing obvious changed, do a quick review once or twice a year to catch expiry, stale contacts and quiet changes.

## What this checklist stops short of

This is for **immediate emergency readiness** — the first minutes and hours when someone needs to find basic household information quickly. It is not a full continuity plan for keeping the household running for days or weeks. It does not try to track every recurring bill, open claim, contractor, digital account, unfinished task or longer-term responsibility.

---

This is a practical preparedness tool, not legal, medical, financial, insurance, privacy, security or emergency-response advice. Follow current instructions from relevant authorities and professionals.
