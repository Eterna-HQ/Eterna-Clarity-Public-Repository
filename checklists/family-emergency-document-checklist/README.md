# Family Emergency Document Checklist

A free checklist for making sure your household can quickly find the information you may need during an emergency.

**[Download the Excel workbook](checklist.xlsx)** · [Open the browser version](CHECKLIST.md)

## What this is

During an emergency, the problem is often not that the information does not exist. It is that nobody can find it quickly.

This checklist helps you make sure the essentials are easy to locate, including:

- emergency contacts and meeting or pickup plans;
- medications, allergies and important health information;
- insurance and coverage details;
- locations of identity and legal documents;
- care information for children, dependants and pets; and
- basic home, utility and evacuation information.

The goal is not to put every important document into one folder. It is to make sure somebody can find the right information when normal routines are disrupted.

## When it helps

Use this checklist before you need it.

It is especially useful when:

- important information is spread across different phones, folders, providers or people;
- you are reviewing your household emergency plan;
- you are moving, travelling or changing care arrangements;
- medications, insurance, school, pet or dependant information has changed; or
- you want to see whether somebody else could find the basics without asking you where everything is.

## What you get

The Excel workbook includes:

- a **Start Here** page;
- the full household emergency checklist;
- review triggers that help you know when information may need updating; and
- source and limitation notes.

The browser version is easy to read, copy or print.

The checklist covers contacts and coordination, health and care, identity and legal information, insurance and financial references, children and dependants, pets, and home and evacuation basics.

## If you only have 15 minutes

Start with the things most likely to matter in the first hours:

1. emergency contacts and your meeting or pickup plan;
2. medications, allergies and essential medical equipment;
3. health coverage and insurance information;
4. where current identity and legal documents are kept;
5. care arrangements for children, dependants or pets; and
6. safe exits and the location of your emergency kit.

That first pass is useful on its own. You can come back for the rest later.

## The ten-minute test

Give somebody you trust the starting point and ten minutes.

Can they find the current contacts, medication information, insurance details, important-document locations and care information **without using your phone and without you explaining where everything is**?

If not, improve the path to the information before simply creating more copies.

## Keep it safe

Do **not** use this checklist to collect passwords, PINs, recovery codes, full credentials or unnecessary account secrets.

Sensitive originals should normally stay in their secure home. If you need quick access to information, a phone number, reference number, short summary or safe pointer may be more useful than carrying another copy of the original document.

## How this differs from the Information Plan

This checklist is about the **first minutes and hours** of an emergency.

The [If Something Happened to Me Information Plan](../../templates/if-something-happened-to-me-information-plan/) is broader. It is designed for the **days and weeks after that**, when somebody may need to understand ongoing situations, recurring responsibilities and where the real household information lives.
