# Family Emergency Document Checklist

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Public review candidate  
**Maintainer:** Eterna Clarity

The Family Emergency Document Checklist is a practical household readiness tool for the information that becomes difficult to find when normal routines break down.

It is not a list of every document a household owns. It uses three different readiness needs:

1. **Know where it is.** Someone appropriate should be able to locate the current or authoritative record.
2. **Keep useful details or a copy accessible.** For some items, a contact route, policy number, short summary, or reference copy is what helps first.
3. **Protect the original.** Sensitive originals and authoritative records should stay in an appropriate secure home unless there is a real reason to move them.

A copy of a passport is not the passport. An insurance summary is not the policy. A medication list does not replace a prescription or medical record. The checklist is designed to make those distinctions visible instead of encouraging one oversized emergency folder full of sensitive originals.

## Use the ten-minute test

Pick a realistic emergency and give an appropriate person ten minutes. Could they find the current contacts, medication information, insurance details, identity-document location, care arrangements, and the few other things that matter most in your household **without relying on your phone or an explanation from you**?

If not, that is the gap to fix.

## Security boundary

Do not use this checklist to collect passwords, PINs, recovery codes, full credentials, or unnecessary account secrets. Do not put every sensitive original into one portable package merely because it is important. Accessibility and protection are separate requirements.

## Scope

This is a preparedness checklist, not legal, medical, financial, insurance, privacy, security, or emergency-response advice. Applicability depends on the household. A listed item does not mean every household must possess it, carry it, copy it, or keep it for a specific period.

Government of Canada emergency-planning guidance supports planning for household contacts, child pickup, health and insurance information, pets, utility locations, medications and supplies, and easy access to essential document copies. Actual emergency instructions come from the relevant authorities and professionals.
