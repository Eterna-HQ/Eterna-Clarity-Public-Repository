# Family Emergency Document Checklist

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Public review candidate  
**Maintainer:** Eterna Clarity

The Family Emergency Document Checklist is a practical household tool for the information that becomes hard to find when normal routines break.

The checklist separates three different needs:

1. **Know where it is.** Someone should be able to locate the current or original record.
2. **Keep the useful part accessible.** A phone number, reference number, short summary or appropriate copy may be what helps in the first hours.
3. **Protect the original.** Sensitive originals should stay in their normal secure home unless there is a real reason to move them.

That distinction prevents an emergency folder from becoming a second archive full of sensitive documents. A passport copy is not the passport. An insurance summary is not the policy. A medication list helps someone understand the current situation, but it does not replace a prescription or medical record.

## Start small

If you only have a few minutes, start with contacts and meeting/pickup plans; medications and allergies; insurance and health information; locations of identity/legal documents; dependant or pet arrangements; and the emergency-kit / exit plan. The full checklist can come later.

## Use the ten-minute test

Give someone you trust the starting point and ten minutes. Can they find the current contacts, medication information, insurance details, identity-document location, care arrangements and the few other things that matter most **without your phone and without you explaining where anything is**?

If not, fix the path to the information before simply creating more copies.

## Immediate readiness, not full household continuity

This resource is deliberately bounded to the first minutes and hours of an emergency. It does not try to document every recurring payment, open claim, contractor, digital account, unfinished task or responsibility that would matter if someone were unavailable for days or weeks. Those require a broader continuity handoff, not a larger emergency checklist.

## Security boundary

Do not use this checklist to collect passwords, PINs, recovery codes, full credentials or unnecessary account secrets. Do not put every sensitive original into one portable package merely because it is important. In free-text fields, record a safe location or access pointer rather than the secret itself.

## Scope and evidence

Applicability depends on the household. A listed item does not mean every household must possess it, carry it, copy it or keep it for a specific period.

Current Government of Canada emergency-planning guidance supports household contacts and meeting plans, child pickup, health and insurance information, medication and medical equipment, emergency kits, banking information, pets/service animals, utility locations, support networks and accessible copies of essential information. Current guidance also supports periodic review of emergency plans and supplies. Actual emergency instructions and legal/medical decisions remain with the relevant authorities and professionals.
