# Sources and limits

Observed 16 September 2026. The worksheet is an original practical synthesis. The following are specific examples of why export, reconstruction and usable access should be distinguished, not a survey of every application's behaviour.

## Official examples

- Notion, [Back up your data](https://www.notion.com/help/back-up-your-data): exporting a workspace does not provide an instant re-upload reconstruction of that workspace. Check the actual scope and recovery route.
- Notion, [Notion Mail inbox is going away](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-mail-inbox-is-going-away-what-to-do-next): the announced September 2026 transition distinguishes synced email history from app-specific drafts, settings and other material. This illustrates why a user should inventory what exists outside the obvious data export. Use the current provider notice for operational dates and instructions.
- Google, [Make someone else the owner of your file](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494892?hl=en): transferring ownership of a folder does not transfer ownership of all files inside it. The intended user's actual access must be checked.

These sources do not endorse this worksheet. Their text is not included in the worksheet's reuse licence. Provider capabilities and policies can change.

## What this does not establish

A completed worksheet is not proof of exhaustive recovery, legal compliance, security, backup durability or a safe account-deletion decision. It does not scan an archive, inspect permissions, transfer ownership or recreate software features. The statuses and counts summarise the user's entries only.

Internal formula, file-structure and presentation checks are not external user testing. No measured time saving, adoption, customer outcome or population-level demand is claimed.
